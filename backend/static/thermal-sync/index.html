    How was this calculated?

    One press starts both cameras together. Emotion, valence and arousal come from the RGB photo or video (MTCNN face detector + EmotiEffLib; for video, one frame every 0.2 s, averaged). Each thermal frame is cropped to the face (found from skin temperature, 30–40 °C), rendered as a white-hot grayscale image and passed to the M3MSTL model (ResNet-50 + Vision Transformer, trained on TFD68), which predicts 68 facial landmarks and whether each is occluded. Temperatures are read from the camera's temperature map at the landmarks and over facial regions. For video, one thermal frame every 0.5 s is analysed. Region values are averaged over the clip, and the slope is a straight-line fit in °C per minute. The two recordings are aligned using the start times of both recorders on this laptop.

    The inner eye corner is the usual stand-in for core temperature in thermal screening. A value of 37.5 °C or more is marked elevated. The TC01A is not a medical thermometer, so treat this as an indicator only. A nose tip that is cooler than the forehead is linked to stress and arousal in published studies.
